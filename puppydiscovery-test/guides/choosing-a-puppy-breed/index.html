Breed fit

How to choose a puppy breed

A good match is about more than appearance. Compare the everyday reality of the breed with the way your household actually lives.

Start with your real routine

Think about a normal weekday, not your ideal weekend. How much time can you realistically give to exercise, training, grooming, supervision, and companionship?

Compare adult size, not puppy size

Every puppy is small at first. Use the breed guide to understand the likely adult size range, then ask about the individual puppy because mixes and individual dogs can vary.

Energy changes the whole experience

High-energy dogs usually need more than a short walk. Consider whether your household can provide regular exercise, training, play, and mental stimulation.

Grooming is a recurring commitment

Long, curly, double, and continuously growing coats can require frequent brushing and professional grooming. Factor the time and cost into the decision before choosing based on coat appearance alone.

Temperament is both breed and individual

Breed tendencies are useful, but they do not predict every dog. Ask what has been observed about the specific puppy’s confidence, handling, play style, and response to people.

Use the breed page, then the puppy page

Start broad with breed fit. Once you find a breed that makes sense, compare the current puppies and ask about the individual dog instead of relying only on the breed label.
