New puppy checklist

Your puppy’s first week at home

The first week does not need to be perfect. A small number of predictable routines usually helps more than trying to teach everything at once.

Keep the first day simple

Give the puppy a calm place to rest, access to water, the same food routine when possible, and frequent bathroom breaks. Too many visitors and too much stimulation can make the first day harder.

Create a feeding and bathroom rhythm

Keep meals predictable and take the puppy out after waking, eating, playing, and before sleep. Frequent opportunities make house training easier than waiting for the puppy to signal perfectly.

Protect sleep

Puppies sleep a lot. Build quiet rest periods into the day and give the puppy a consistent place to settle.

Schedule veterinary follow-up

Follow the seller’s written instructions and arrange any required post-purchase examination promptly. Bring the records you received with the puppy.

Start training with very small wins

Name response, coming a few steps, calm handling, short crate sessions, and rewarding bathroom success are enough to begin. Keep sessions brief and positive.

Expect an adjustment period

Changes in sleep, appetite, confidence, and bathroom habits can happen during a move. Keep notes and contact a veterinarian promptly if you are concerned about the puppy’s health.
