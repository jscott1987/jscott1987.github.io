How Much Does Health Insurance Cost Per Month

Oct 28, 2025

OEH

Learn what Open Enrollment is who it applies to why it matters and how to choose a health plan that fits your budget. Includes a checklist plus next steps if you miss the deadline.

Introduction

One of the most common questions people ask during Open Enrollment is how much health insurance actually costs per month. The answer depends on several factors including where you live your age your income and the type of coverage you choose. This guide breaks down what goes into the monthly cost and how to estimate what you might pay.


Average monthly cost of health insurance

Monthly health insurance costs vary widely. Some people pay relatively low premiums while others pay significantly more depending on their situation.

In general monthly cost is influenced by
Age
Location
Household size
Income level
Plan type and coverage level

Two people living in different states or with different incomes can see very different prices for similar coverage.

What affects the cost of health insurance

Health insurance pricing is not random. Several key factors determine what you pay each month.

Age

Premiums typically increase as you get older. Younger individuals often pay less while older individuals pay more due to higher expected healthcare use.

Location

Where you live matters. Healthcare costs provider availability and competition between plans vary by state and even by county.

Income and household size

Your estimated household income and size can impact eligibility for cost reductions or savings. Accurate income estimates help avoid overpaying or future adjustments.

Plan type

Different plans come with different cost structures. Some have lower monthly premiums but higher deductibles while others cost more monthly but offer more predictable expenses.

Premium versus total cost

The monthly premium is only part of the picture. A plan with a low premium may cost more overall if you use healthcare services frequently.

When estimating cost consider
Monthly premium
Deductible
Copays and coinsurance
Out of pocket maximum

A good comparison looks at what you might spend over the full year not just each month.

How to estimate your monthly cost

To get a realistic estimate
Start with your ZIP code
Estimate household income as accurately as possible
Think about how often you use healthcare
List prescriptions and preferred doctors

These details help narrow down plans that fit both your budget and your needs.

Common mistakes when thinking about cost

Many people focus only on the lowest monthly price. Common mistakes include
Ignoring deductibles
Not checking prescription costs
Overlooking provider networks
Choosing a plan that does not match actual usage

A slightly higher premium can sometimes lead to lower overall costs.

Closing

Health insurance cost per month depends on personal factors and plan design. Understanding how pricing works helps you make a decision that fits your finances without sacrificing coverage you may need later.

Affordable Care Act

Affordable Care Act (ACA) is an extensive healthcare reform sometimes referred to as Obamacare. As per ACA, individual medical insurance plans must cover ten Essential Health Benefits in order to be recognized as a Qualified Health Plan (QHP)and provide “minimum essential coverage (MEC)” to enrollees. To find more information on QHP options in your state, visit www.HealthCare.gov .Premium tax credits are subject to eligibility requirements.

Short Term Medical (STM)

Comprehensive medical coverage is not provided by STM Insurance plans. STM doesn’t fall under the minimum essential coverage as defined by the ACA and may not cover all Essential Health Benefits. Plans typically don’t cover preexisting conditions and may have financial limitations on health benefits.

Fixed Indemnity/Limited Indemnity Plans

Indemnity Plans are complementary to health insurance plans, not a replacement for insurance or a substitute for the minimum essential coverage required by the ACA. If you are eligible for Medicaid, check if these payments affect your coverage and eligibility.
